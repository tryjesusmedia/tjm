Understand Bible Prophecy: Daniel and Revelation

What do Daniel and Revelation say about history, Jesus, and the future? This Bible prophecy series examines the passages behind familiar questions, including the identity of the Messiah, the Antichrist, and the mark of the beast.

Questions covered in the nine guides

Start with Daniel’s picture of world kingdoms and the promised Messiah. Then explore worship, authority, religious liberty, and the ways Christians understand the final events in Revelation. The guides present a biblical case for you to examine, with Scripture references and questions for reflection.

  1. Can the Bible predict the future?

    Study Daniel 2, its sequence of kingdoms, and the promise of God’s everlasting kingdom.

  2. Does Daniel point to Jesus?

    Follow the messianic timeline in Daniel 9 and examine the biblical and historical evidence presented.

  3. How do law and grace fit together?

    Explore God’s character and law as a foundation for understanding worship and prophecy. This guide also appears in Get to Know Jesus.

  4. Who is the Antichrist?

    Compare the identifying characteristics described in Daniel and Revelation before evaluating an interpretation.

  5. The seal of God and the mark of the beast

    Investigate the passages about worship, authority, and allegiance in Revelation’s final conflict.

  6. Is America in Bible prophecy?

    Examine the interpretation of Revelation 13 presented in the guide, including its connection to religious liberty and church-state power.

  7. Why are there so many churches?

    Trace the development of Christian traditions and consider a biblical framework for evaluating a church.

  8. How can you recognize God’s church?

    Study Revelation’s description of God’s faithful people and examine the Seventh-day Adventist interpretation discussed in the guide.

  9. How should a prophet be tested?

    Consider biblical tests of prophetic claims, the 1844 question, and the ministry of Ellen G. White.

Study the passages before choosing an interpretation

Prophecy involves symbols, historical claims, and different interpretations. These guides invite you to examine the evidence rather than accept a conclusion because it is familiar. Read surrounding verses, compare the passages used in each lesson, and bring unresolved questions to the weekly discussion.
