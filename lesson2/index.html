A WORLD WORTH RESTORING

The Stolen Masterpiece

In 1985, thieves entered the University of Arizona Museum of Art and cut Willem de Kooning’s Woman-Ochre from its frame.

For almost 32 years, the painting was missing. When it was recovered, the canvas had been cut, rolled, and badly damaged.

But the damage did not erase its identity. It was still a masterpiece.

Experts spent years studying and restoring it before it returned to public view.

CREATEDBeautiful
STOLENDamaged
RESTOREDMade New

Our world is also damaged. Violence, disease, betrayal, injustice, and death were not part of the masterpiece God originally designed.

The story of the Bible is not the story of a Creator throwing away a ruined world. It is the story of a Creator restoring what evil tried to steal.

The painting was stolen on November 29, 1985, recovered in New Mexico in August 2017, and sent to the Getty for years of technical study and conservation. It returned to the University of Arizona Museum of Art in 2022.

Restoration did not create its value. It was restored because it was already valuable.

Is Everything God’s Will?

People often respond to tragedy with a familiar sentence:

A COMMON ASSUMPTION

Was the betrayal His plan? Was the abuse His will? Did He design the cruel decision, the collision, or the violence?

THE PRICE OF REAL RELATIONSHIP

The Risk Built Into Love

Imagine technology capable of programming another person to love you.

After it was activated, that person would admire you, agree with you, and never leave you.

Would that be love?

PROGRAMMED RESPONSE

Predictable behavior without meaningful choice.

REAL LOVE

Freely given trust with the possibility of rejection.

THE CHARACTER OF GOD1 John 4:8Tap to open

Full text shown in the public-domain KJV. Read the NKJV on BibleGateway ↗

He that loveth not knoweth not God; for God is love.

Love cannot be programmed. It cannot be forced. It cannot exist without the possibility of rejection.

God has the power to control behavior, but controlled behavior is not loving devotion.

The power to say yes means very little when there is no possibility of saying no.

Open the programmed-love illustration

Imagine that you possess a device capable of controlling another person’s mind. You program someone to adore you. They praise you constantly, never disagree, and never leave.

But who created every affectionate response? You did.

Their “love” is only your own programming returning to you. That is not relationship. It is control disguised as devotion.

God could have created beings incapable of disobedience. But beings incapable of rejecting Him would also be incapable of freely trusting Him.

CONSIDER

Would love still be love if rejection were impossible?

Why didn’t God destroy the rebel immediately?

Imagine a universe that had never witnessed deceit, selfishness, or death. If God had immediately destroyed the first being who questioned His government, the remaining beings might have obeyed—but from fear.

The rebel accused God’s authority of being restrictive and untrustworthy. Immediate destruction could have appeared to confirm the accusation.

God allowed rebellion time to reveal its nature. History shows where separation from God leads: exploitation, domination, violence, suffering, and death.

At the cross, God revealed the opposite character. He did not preserve His authority by sacrificing His creatures. He sacrificed Himself to save them.

What about verses that seem to say God causes evil?

Isaiah 45:7

The KJV uses the word “evil,” while the context contrasts peace with calamity. The passage concerns historical judgment, not God creating moral wickedness.

Who moved David to number Israel?

Second Samuel 24:1 attributes the event to God’s sovereign judgment; First Chronicles 21:1 identifies Satan as the active instigator. Permission is not the same as moral authorship.

Job’s suffering

God permitted Satan to test Job and placed limits around what Satan could do. Satan caused the suffering; God remained sovereign over its boundaries and ultimately brought restoration.

God Entered the Suffering

Jesus did not merely explain the conflict. He stepped into it.

Open the “too precious to leave behind” illustration

Imagine a child bringing a favorite action figure to school against the rules. While hiding it, the toy falls into filthy water.

The child could walk away. Instead, he reaches in. The toy is too precious to abandon. He retrieves it, cleans it, and restores it.

When humanity fell into the contamination of sin, Jesus did not consider us too dirty to touch. He entered our condition, became human, and carried our suffering and sin to the cross because we were too precious to leave behind.
