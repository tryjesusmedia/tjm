THE FRACTURE BENEATH THE SYMPTOMS

When the Connection Was Broken

The Bible begins with a world described as “very good.” Humanity lived in harmony with God, with one another, and with creation.

Then trust was broken.

Human beings accepted the suggestion that life could be fuller, freer, and more advanced apart from God.

Full text shown in the public-domain KJV. Read the NKJV on BibleGateway ↗

But your iniquities have separated between you and your God, and your sins have hid his face from you, that he will not hear.

Sin is often presented as a list of prohibited behaviors. But Scripture describes something deeper.

Sin is separation from the source of life, love, truth, and goodness.

It is the attempt to build an independent life while disconnected from the One who gave us life.

DesiresDecisionsRelationshipsIdentitySocietyFuture

The visible problems are symptoms.

The broken connection is the disease.

See how one simple problem created a global solution

In 2008, Brian Chesky and Joe Gebbia were struggling to afford their San Francisco apartment. A major conference was coming to the city, and local hotels were nearly full.

Visitors needed somewhere to stay. They had available space. Their early solution was simple: air mattresses, a place to sleep, and breakfast.

That modest experiment eventually developed into Airbnb.

The comparison has limits, but the principle is useful: the value of a solution becomes clear only when the real problem has been identified.

If humanity's problem were only ignorance, education would be enough. If it were only poor habits, discipline would be enough. If it were only difficult circumstances, opportunity would be enough.

But if the problem is separation from the source of life, we need reconciliation and a new life.

THE LIMIT OF DISCIPLINE

Why Self-Improvement Is Not Enough

Most people recognize at least part of what is wrong.

We know we should be more patient, more honest, more generous, more disciplined, and more loving.

The difficulty is not always knowing what to do.

It is becoming the kind of person who consistently desires and does it.

What did humanity inherit?

The Bible teaches that every person enters a world already damaged by sin. We inherit mortality, disordered desires, broken social systems, and patterns shaped by generations before us.

A BROKEN HUMAN CONDITIONPsalm 51:5Tap to open

Full text shown in the public-domain KJV. Read the NKJV on BibleGateway ↗

Behold, I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me.

This does not mean an infant is personally guilty of deliberate rebellion. It means no one begins life in the unbroken environment experienced at creation.

We enter mortality, self-centered impulses, damaged relationships, unjust systems, and a creation subject to decay. We then add our own choices to the problem.

The biblical conclusion is not that some people are naturally superior. It is that every person needs grace.

Why couldn't God simply ignore sin?

Forgiveness is sometimes pictured as God pretending nothing serious happened. But love does not ignore destruction.

A leader who ignores corruption is not merciful. A spouse who calls betrayal harmless is not loving. A judge who has no concern for the victim is not just.

God takes sin seriously because He takes people seriously. Sin destroys the very people He loves.

RESCUE WITHOUT COERCION

The Offer Love Will Not Force

Imagine that God warned humanity not to step from the edge of a cliff.

The deceiver replied:

THE PROMISE

“God is restricting you. Beyond the edge is freedom. You will discover that you can fly.”

Humanity steps over.

The promise of flight becomes the reality of falling.

Jesus does not remain safely above and shout instructions.

He enters the fall.

He catches humanity and offers to bear the consequence.

But rescue still requires trust.

Open the complete cliff-and-rescue illustration

God warns: “Do not step beyond this boundary. It leads to death.”

The deceiver responds: “God is holding you back. Beyond the edge is a higher existence.”

Humanity steps over. The promised freedom becomes a fall.

Jesus does not stand at the top and merely demand that humanity climb back. He enters the fall, catches humanity, and offers to bear the consequence.

The person being held can stop resisting and accept life—or continue demanding the freedom to fall.

The illustration shows why salvation includes both divine action and human response. Jesus provides the rescue no one else could provide. But love does not force the rescued person to remain in His hands.

Read the Howard Schultz story about conviction and trust

In the 1980s, Howard Schultz saw potential in the coffeehouse experience that the original owners of Starbucks did not initially share.

He imagined something larger than selling coffee beans: a welcoming “third place” between home and work where people could pause, meet, and connect.

Pursuing that vision required leaving security, raising money, and hearing repeated rejection from investors. Schultz eventually developed his own coffee concept and later acquired Starbucks, helping build it into a global company.

The comparison with the gospel has limits. Jesus did not merely risk professional security; He knowingly gave Himself to rescue humanity.

But both stories confront us with a decision about trust. Investors had to decide whether Schultz's vision deserved their confidence. Every person must decide whether Jesus deserves something far greater: the direction of his or her life.

Faith is not merely admiring Jesus from a distance. It is entrusting ourselves to the One who loved us and gave Himself for us.

THE DECISION BENEATH EVERY DECISION

What Are You Really Trusting?

The deepest human problem is not a lack of intelligence, opportunity, or ambition.

It is separation from God—and the self-centered life that grows from it.

The solution is not simply trying harder.

It is receiving the life of Jesus.

OPTION ONEKeep managing the symptoms

Build an admirable life while remaining spiritually disconnected.

OPTION TWOReceive a life you cannot manufacture

Allow Jesus to address the source, not merely the appearance.

Jesus has already made the sacrifice.

The question is whether you will receive what He offers.
