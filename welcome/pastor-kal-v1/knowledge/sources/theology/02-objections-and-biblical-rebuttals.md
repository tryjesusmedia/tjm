# Major Objections to law of love and life — and the Biblical Rebuttal

The easiest way to understand the objections is to recognize that almost all of them arise from one underlying question:

**What kind of law does God govern His universe by?**

If God's eternal moral law functions primarily like human legislation—rules established by an authority that must then impose penalties—sin creates primarily a **legal problem**, and salvation must contain a legal solution.

If God's eternal law consists of the Creator's **design protocols for life**, sin creates primarily a **real, lethal problem in the sinner**, and salvation must actually cure that problem.

law of love and life does not deny that Scripture contains commandments, courts, judges, condemnation, wrath, punishment, sacrifice, blood, judgment, and legal metaphors. It argues that those concepts must ultimately be interpreted in harmony with the deeper reality that **God is Creator, God is love, God is the Source of life, sin damages His creatures, and salvation restores them to harmony with Him.** The framework also distinguishes God's eternal law of love and lifes from temporary added laws given to sinful humanity for protection, diagnosis, education, and civil order. ([Come And Reason Ministries](https://comeandreason.com/law-gives-life-and-freedom/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

# OBJECTION 1: “The Bible clearly says God gives commands. Therefore His laws are imposed laws.”

This sounds initially decisive.

God says:

- “You shall not…”
- “You shall…”
- “Keep My commandments.”
- “Obey My voice.”
- “If you disobey…”

Therefore, the objection says, God's law obviously operates like legislation.

## Rebuttal

**Giving a command does not establish that the underlying reality is created by the command.**

A physician can command:

> “Do not drink this poison.”

The command is imposed verbally, but the danger is not imposed.

The poison would harm the patient whether or not the physician issued the command.

Likewise, God can issue verbal commands describing and protecting His creatures from violations of reality.

The deeper question is not:

**“Did God issue commandments?”**

Of course He did.

The question is:

**“Why are those things right or wrong?”**

Does murder become destructive only because God issued Exodus 20:13?

No.

Murder violates love, destroys life, damages relationships, corrupts character, and opposes the character of the Creator.

Jesus therefore takes the commandments beneath the external wording to the underlying heart principle.

Murder → hatred.

Adultery → lust.

False obedience → selfish motives.

**Matthew 5:21–48**

And then Jesus summarizes the entire law:

**Matthew 22:37–40**
Love God completely and love your neighbor as yourself.

Paul makes precisely the same move:

**Romans 13:8–10**

> Love does no harm to a neighbor; therefore love is the fulfillment of the law.

The written commandments are therefore **expressions and applications of the deeper law of love**.

# OBJECTION 2: “If law of love and life is true, why did God give the Ten Commandments at Sinai?”

Because fallen human beings needed them.

The eternal law of love existed before Sinai.

Cain murdered Abel long before the command:

**“You shall not murder.”**

Joseph knew adultery was sinful before Sinai:

**Genesis 39:9**

Satan sinned before Sinai.

Adam sinned before Sinai.

Therefore sin cannot fundamentally mean violating a document delivered on Mount Sinai.

The written law made explicit what fallen minds were increasingly unable to perceive.

Paul actually says:

**Galatians 3:19**

> “Why then was the law given at all? It was added because of transgressions…”

And:

**1 Timothy 1:9**

> “The law is not made for a righteous person, but for the lawless and insubordinate…”

The written law functions diagnostically and protectively.

Romans 7:7:

> “I would not have known what coveting really was if the law had not said, ‘You shall not covet.’”

So the commandment identifies the disorder.

Christ heals the disorder.

The the law of love and life understanding therefore distinguishes the **eternal principle** from its **written expression adapted to sinful humanity**. ([Come And Reason Ministries](https://comeandreason.com/files/ss_lessons-2023/2023_Q2_L03_notes.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Sin is simultaneously:

**rebellion, lawlessness, distrust, selfishness, corruption, bondage, sickness, and separation from God.**

The Bible itself uses disease and healing language repeatedly.

Isaiah describes sinful humanity:

**Isaiah 1:5–6**

The whole head is sick. The whole heart is faint.

David prays:

**Psalm 51:10**

> “Create in me a clean heart.”

God promises:

**Ezekiel 36:26**

> “I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you.”

Jesus describes sinners as needing a physician:

**Mark 2:17**

> “It is not the healthy who need a doctor, but the sick.”

Peter says:

**1 Peter 2:24**

> “By His wounds you have been healed.”

The disease analogy therefore does not soften sin.

It makes sin **more serious**.

A legal infraction can potentially be pardoned without changing the offender.

A terminal condition must actually be cured.

- birth
- resurrection
- healing
- cleansing
- adoption
- liberation
- reconciliation
- creation
- marriage
- redemption
- transformation
- participation
- grafting
- receiving a new heart

No single metaphor exhausts salvation.

When Scripture says:

**2 Corinthians 5:17**

> “If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation,”

When God says:

**Ezekiel 36:26**

> “I will give you a new heart,”

When Paul says:

**Romans 12:2**

something real happens to the sinner.

God does not simply falsify the record of an unchanged sinner.

He joins that sinner to Christ and begins restoring that person to righteousness.

# OBJECTION 10: “Isaiah 53 says God punished Jesus.”

Isaiah 53 is one of the strongest passages raised against law of love and life.

But notice something extremely important.

Isaiah says:

**Isaiah 53:4**

> “We considered Him punished by God, stricken by Him, and afflicted.”

That describes **what humans thought was happening**.

The chapter then describes Jesus:

- bearing our griefs
- carrying our sorrows
- being wounded because of our transgressions
- bearing our iniquities
- pouring out His life
- bearing the sins of many

law of love and life affirms every one.

The question is whether “bearing sin” means:

**God transferred our legal punishment onto Jesus**

or:

**Christ took upon Himself our humanity, entered the consequences of the sin condition, carried our sin burden, conquered sin, and provided the cure.**

Peter interprets Isaiah 53 this way:

**1 Peter 2:24**

> “He Himself bore our sins in His body on the cross…”

Why?

The verse continues:

> “so that we might **die to sins and live for righteousness**.”

And then:

> “by His wounds **you have been healed**.”

The objective is not simply changing God's legal attitude toward sinners.

It is healing sinners so they live righteousness.

# OBJECTION 11: “2 Corinthians 5:21 says Jesus became sin for us. Isn't that penal substitution?”

The verse says:

> “God made Him who had no sin to be sin for us, so that **in Him we might become the righteousness of God**.”

Notice both halves.

Christ enters our sin condition.

We enter His righteousness.

But Jesus did not literally become morally sinful.

Our individual guilty deeds were not biologically inserted into Christ.

“Became sin” therefore already involves representative/substitutionary language.

The question is what Christ accomplished through that identification.

Paul's answer culminates not merely in our avoiding punishment but in our **becoming God's righteousness in Christ**.

And the immediately preceding verses describe the cross as **reconciliation**:

**2 Corinthians 5:18–19**

> “God was reconciling the world to Himself in Christ, not counting people's sins against them.”

The direction of reconciliation matters.

God is reconciling humanity **to Himself**.

He is not using Christ to reconcile Himself to humanity.

Jesus says:

**Mark 10:45**

Ransom language describes the **cost of liberation**.

Christ gives Himself to free humanity from:

- sin
- death
- corruption
- Satan's dominion
- slavery
- fear

**Hebrews 2:14–15**

The sacrifice is real.

The cost is infinite.

# OBJECTION 14: “Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness.”

Hebrews 9:22 is frequently treated as if it says:

> “God cannot legally forgive unless somebody's blood is paid to Him.”

But read the actual verse:

> “In fact, the law requires that **nearly everything** be cleansed with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness.”

Notice **cleansing**.

And notice “nearly.”

The Old Testament itself contains an atonement offering where a poor person could bring **flour rather than blood**, and the text says:

> “The priest shall make atonement…and they will be forgiven.”

**Leviticus 5:11–13**

So Hebrews cannot mean that God possesses an absolute metaphysical inability to forgive unless blood physically flows.

Blood symbolizes **life**.

**Leviticus 17:11**

> “The life of a creature is in the blood.”

And significantly God says:

> “I have **given it to you** to make atonement…”

God gives the remedy.

Humanity does not devise a blood payment to persuade God to become merciful.

Hebrews' own emphasis is cleansing:

**Hebrews 9:14**

Christ's blood cleanses our consciences.

**Hebrews 10:19–22**

We approach with cleansed hearts.

The sacrificial system therefore points toward the life of Christ given to **cleanse, restore, redeem, and reconcile**.

# OBJECTION 15: “Propitiation means God's wrath had to be appeased.”

This is one of the most important objections.

Romans 3:25 and 1 John 2:2 use the Greek *hilastērion/hilasmos* family, frequently translated “propitiation.”

Penal-substitution advocates understand this as Christ satisfying or turning away God's judicial wrath. ([The Gospel Coalition](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/blogs/kevin-deyoung/place-condemned-stood/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

But Hebrews 9:5 uses *hilastērion* for the **mercy seat**—the atonement cover over the ark.

So the word itself does not require the picture:

> Angry God + blood payment → appeased God.

Furthermore, Scripture explicitly says God's love precedes the sacrifice.

**John 3:16**

God loved → therefore God gave.

Not:

Christ died → therefore God loved.

**Romans 5:8**

> “God demonstrates His own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us.”

**1 John 4:10**

God's love initiates the entire saving mission.

Thus Christ's sacrifice deals with the sin barrier while originating entirely within God's preexisting love.

# OBJECTION 18: “If God simply forgives, why did Jesus need to die?”

This is perhaps the most important rebuttal of all.

Because **forgiveness alone cannot heal a terminally corrupted humanity**.

Imagine a dying patient saying:

> “Doctor, do you forgive me for destroying my liver?”

The doctor answers:

> “Completely.”

Wonderful.

But the patient still needs a new liver.

Forgiveness addresses relational hostility.

It does not automatically reverse physiological destruction.

Likewise God could love sinners, forgive sinners, and still need to accomplish something objectively necessary **inside humanity itself**.

That is why Christ became the second Adam.

Romans 8:3:

> God sent His Son “in the likeness of sinful flesh…And so He condemned sin in the flesh.”

Hebrews 2:14:

> Since humans share flesh and blood, **He too shared in their humanity**.

1 Corinthians 15:21:

> “Since death came through a man, the resurrection of the dead comes also through a man.”

Humanity needed to be restored from within humanity.

Therefore Christ's incarnation, life, death, and resurrection were indispensable. ([Come And Reason Ministries](https://comeandreason.com/were-crucifixion-death-of-christ-necessary/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Isaiah 33:14**

**Daniel 7:9–10**

**Revelation 22:3–5**

# OBJECTION 24: “What about the fear of punishment keeping people obedient?”

Fear can control behavior.

But fear cannot create Christlike character.

Fear asks:

> “What will happen to me?”

Love asks:

> “What is good, true, and loving?”

That distinction is central.

A person may avoid adultery because:

> “God will punish me.”

Another may avoid adultery because:

> “I love my spouse and would never want to betray or injure them.”

Outward behavior might initially look identical.

Character is radically different.

God's goal is not frightened compliance.

It is mature love.

**1 John 4:18**
Perfect love removes punishment-fear.

**Galatians 5:6**
What matters is faith working through love.

**Romans 13:10**
Love fulfills the law.

**Jeremiah 31:33**
God writes His law inside us.

# OBJECTION 26: “law of love and life makes human freedom more powerful than God's sovereignty.”

No.

God sovereignly chose to create beings capable of authentic love.

That sovereign choice necessarily includes giving them meaningful agency.

# The Most Important Distinction

A very effective way to answer objections to law of love and life is to ask:

> **Is sin bad because God prohibited it, or did God prohibit it because it is inherently contrary to love, life, truth, freedom, and His design for creation?**

If something becomes sinful merely because God issues a rule against it, morality ultimately rests upon **divine power and decree**.

But if God commands what He commands because His commands faithfully express **His own eternal character and the reality He created**, morality rests upon who God actually is.

Then God's commandments become extraordinarily beautiful.

They are not:

> **“Obey Me or I will hurt you.”**

They are God's loving warning:

> **“I created you. I know how life works. Trust Me. This is the way of love, freedom, health, righteousness, and life. The other way will destroy you. I love you too much to leave you there, so I have come in Christ to rescue you, heal you, restore you, and bring you home.”**

That is the heart of the the law of love and life rebuttal.

And it explains why perhaps the most important new-covenant promise in the entire Bible is not:

> “I will change their heavenly legal records.”

It is:

> **“I will put My laws in their minds and write them on their hearts.” — Hebrews 8:10**

God's final answer to sin is not merely to **forgive lawbreakers while leaving them broken**.

His answer is to **re-create His children in the image of Christ so thoroughly that His law of love once again becomes the principle upon which they naturally live.** ([Come And Reason Ministries](https://comeandreason.com/imputed-and-imparted-righteousness-design-vs-imposed-law/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
