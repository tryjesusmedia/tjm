Get to Know Jesus Through Bible Study

Who is Jesus, and what does knowing Him mean for everyday life? This series explores the questions that often stand between curiosity and faith: why a loving God allows suffering, why people need salvation, and what the Bible says about death and hope.

What you will study

The guides follow a connected journey. They begin with God’s character and the problem of evil, then explore salvation through the sanctuary, the ministry of Jesus, and the Bible’s picture of the future. Each lesson includes Scripture references, explanations, and questions for personal reflection.

  1. Why does God allow evil?

    Explore human freedom, the origin of evil, and what the cross reveals about God’s response to suffering.

  2. Why do we need salvation?

    Consider the spiritual problem that achievement cannot solve and the new life Jesus offers.

  3. God’s law and grace

    Examine how God’s law relates to love, freedom, and character, and why grace matters.

  4. The sanctuary and salvation

    Discover how the sanctuary’s services and symbols point to Jesus and reconciliation with God.

  5. What is Jesus doing now?

    Study the Bible’s description of Christ’s ministry in heaven and what it means for faith today.

  6. The Day of Atonement

    Explore the connections between forgiveness, cleansing, accountability, and judgment.

  7. What happens when you die?

    Read the passages about death, consciousness, resurrection, and the hope found in Jesus.

  8. Is hell eternal?

    Examine the Bible’s language of fire, destruction, justice, and the final end of evil.

  9. The millennium in Revelation

    Follow the sequence of events surrounding the thousand years and the promise of a restored world.

  10. What is true worship?

    Consider how everyday choices, relationships, and habits express worship beyond a church service.

Begin with your questions

You do not need to have everything settled before you begin. Read the Bible references in context, compare the passages, and reflect on the explanation offered in each guide. Move through the lessons at your own pace, returning to difficult questions as you learn.
