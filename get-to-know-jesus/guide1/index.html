The stolen masterpiece

Damage Does Not Cancel Ownership

In 1985, thieves cut Willem de Kooning’s Woman-Ochre from its frame. It disappeared for thirty-two years.

1985
The painting is stolen

The canvas is cut from its frame and badly damaged.

2017
The work resurfaces

Antique-shop owners recognize its importance and return it to the museum.

2018–22
Restoration begins

Conservators stabilize the paint, repair the canvas, and recover the artist’s work.

THE CLUE
Value survived the damage

The masterpiece had been violated—but it had not ceased to belong to its maker.

Creation began as a masterpiece. Something invaded it. Something corrupted it. The Artist did not walk away.

THE QUESTION BENEATH THE QUESTION If our world is damaged, is God the one who damaged it—or the One working to restore it?

The cost of genuine love

Why Does God Not Simply Stop Every Wrong Choice?

The Bible does not present everything happening on earth as God’s will.

LOVE REQUIRES
  • Meaningful choice
  • The ability to trust
  • The freedom to refuse
  • Character formed without coercion
VS
CONTROL PRODUCES
  • Programmed behavior
  • Outward compliance
  • Fear of punishment
  • An imitation of relationship

Imagine possessing technology capable of programming someone to love you. They would admire you, remain loyal, and never leave. But their affection would only be your own programming reflected back to you.

Love can be invited, demonstrated, and chosen. It cannot be manufactured by force.

Is free will a complete answer?+

No. Freedom helps explain moral evil, but it does not by itself explain every disease, disaster, or apparently undeserved tragedy. This guide also considers a creation damaged by sin and the Bible’s larger spiritual conflict.

The rebellion before Eden

Evil Did Not Begin With God

The Bible presents evil as an intruder—not an eternal force equal to God.

01
A magnificent created being

Ezekiel describes one who was “perfect” until corruption developed within him.

02
A private ambition

Isaiah exposes the repeated desire: “I will ascend… I will exalt… I will be like the Most High.”

03
A campaign of mistrust

Like Absalom stealing Israel’s loyalty, rebellion begins by persuading others that the King cannot be trusted.

04
An open conflict

Revelation describes war in heaven and identifies the dragon as Satan, the deceiver.

THE ORIGIN OF EVILA created mind corrupted freedom through pride, self-exaltation, and mistrust of God.

Do Ezekiel 28 and Isaiah 14 directly describe Satan?+

In their immediate settings, Ezekiel addresses the ruler of Tyre and Isaiah addresses the king of Babylon. Many Christians understand the language as reaching beyond those rulers to the spiritual pride and rebellion they embodied. Adventist interpreters connect these passages with Revelation 12.

That connection is an interpretation drawn from the combined biblical pattern—not something the text states in a single sentence.

Why God did not end it immediately

Power Can Silence an Accuser—Only Truth Can Answer an Accusation

The universe had never seen betrayal, violence, disease, or death. No one had witnessed the final result of separating from God.

THE REBEL’S WAY
  • Reach upward for status
  • Question the King’s motives
  • Present boundaries as control
  • Use freedom for self-exaltation
→
CHRIST’S WAY
  • Step downward to serve
  • Reveal the Father’s character
  • Absorb the cost of rescue
  • Use power for self-giving love

If the rebel had been destroyed immediately, the conflict might have ended—but the questions behind it could have remained. Was God threatened by scrutiny? Was His law designed for love or control? Did He rule through goodness or fear?

God is ending rebellion in a way that leaves no unanswered lie to poison the future.

What this framework can—and cannot—answer+

The great-conflict framework says evil is an intruder, God is not its author, creation is caught in a conflict of wills, and Jesus enters the conflict rather than observing it from a distance.

It is not a slogan that makes every loss emotionally easy or every tragedy intellectually transparent.

Why innocent people suffer

Not Every Tragedy Is a Punishment

Jesus rejected the assumption that victims of tragedy must have been worse sinners than everyone else.

01Human choices

Violence, betrayal, exploitation, neglect, and abuse wound people God loves.

02A damaged creation

Disease, genetic disorders, natural disasters, aging, and death reveal a world “groaning.”

03A spiritual conflict

Scripture recognizes unseen evil, while never authorizing us to blame a specific tragedy on a demon.

A NECESSARY RESTRAINT

Not every painful event has a hidden lesson. Not everything “happens for a reason” in the sense that God personally designed it. But nothing places you beyond His presence or His power to redeem.

What should you never say to someone in pain?+
  • “This must have happened because of your sin.”
  • “Everything happened exactly according to God’s plan.”
  • “Your faith would have prevented this.”
  • “You should be over it by now.”

Jesus often responded to suffering with presence, compassion, healing, and practical care before offering an explanation.

Permission, judgment, and redemption

God Can Redeem What He Did Not Author

Some Bible passages sound as though God creates evil. Careful comparison reveals an important distinction.

01
A creature chooses

Human or spiritual agents act from their own motives.

02
God permits and limits

Permission is not the same as sharing the agent’s character.

03
God judges evil

Calamity in judgment is not the creation of moral wickedness.

04
God creates a redemptive outcome

He can bring rescue from what another person intended for harm.

Joseph did not call betrayal good. He declared that evil intentions did not have the authority to control the ending.

Does Isaiah 45:7 say God creates moral evil?+

The NKJV translates the term as “calamity.” The context concerns God’s sovereignty over nations and historical judgment—not God creating deceit, cruelty, abuse, or sinful desire within people.

THE CROSS CHANGES THE QUESTION God did not explain pain from a safe distance. He entered it.

Jesus experienced hunger, exhaustion, rejection, betrayal, injustice, grief, physical agony, and death. The cross does not make every tragedy understandable. It makes God’s character unmistakable.

The masterpiece will be restored

Evil Has an Expiration Date

The Bible does not end with humanity escaping an abandoned world. It ends with God restoring what was damaged.

WHAT YOU HAVE DISCOVERED
  • God did not create evil.
  • Love requires meaningful freedom.
  • Freedom can be corrupted by pride.
  • Not everything that happens is God’s will.
  • Not every tragedy is a punishment.
  • Creation itself is waiting for restoration.
  • God can redeem events He did not design.
  • The cross reveals a God who enters suffering.
PRIVATE REFLECTION

When you picture God, do you see the character revealed by Jesus—or a distant ruler you have blamed for everything that went wrong?

Prayer: Jesus, I do not understand every painful thing that has happened. Help me see Your character clearly. Heal what has been damaged within me. I entrust my past, my questions, and my future to You. Restore in me what evil has tried to steal. Amen.

What this guide is not claiming+
  • That every painful event can be fully explained.
  • That every tragedy was deliberately planned by God.
  • That disease is proof of a particular sin or weak faith.
  • That grief disappears when someone learns the correct theology.
  • That Christians should remain passive in the presence of injustice.

Faith does not require pretending pain is painless. It means learning to trust God’s character while some questions remain unanswered.
