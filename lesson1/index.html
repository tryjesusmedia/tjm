Can an ancient prophecy give us credible reasons to trust the Bible?

WHY THIS MATTERS TO ME

I Did Not Want a Borrowed Faith

My parents’ religion was not enough for me.

I am naturally skeptical, and before I believe something, I want reasons. Daniel 2 became one of the strongest reasons I chose to become a Christian.

But evidence alone did not keep me close to God.

I once served Him as an evangelist. Later, I walked away and pursued what the world offered. Eventually, God called me back.

He showed me that the world could distract me, but it could not satisfy me or change my heart.

Daniel 2 gave me evidence that God is real.

Jesus showed me that He can forgive, restore, and transform a human life.

THE CRISIS

A Problem Human Wisdom Could Not Solve

Nebuchadnezzar, king of Babylon, had a dream that disturbed him deeply. By morning, he could not clearly remember it.

He ordered his wise men to tell him two things: what he had dreamed and what the dream meant.

When they could not answer, the king sentenced Babylon’s wise men to death—including Daniel and his three friends.

Daniel asked for time. Then he and his friends prayed.

READ Daniel 2:17–19 Tap to open

Daniel returned to his house and explained the matter to Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah. He urged them to plead for mercy from the God of heaven concerning the mystery, so they would not be executed with the other wise men of Babylon.

That night, God revealed the mystery. Daniel did not take the credit. He pointed to “the God in heaven who reveals mysteries.”

READ Daniel 2:27–28 Tap to open

Daniel explained that no wise person could reveal the mystery—but that there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries and who had shown the king what would happen.

CONSIDER

When human intelligence could not solve the problem, where did Daniel turn?

Explore the historical evidence

How Babylon Fell

Cyrus II captured Babylon in 539 BC. Ancient sources agree on the conquest, while later accounts give additional details about the military strategy. Isaiah 45 identifies Cyrus by name and pictures doors opening before him.

The prophecy is not saying that European nations will never cooperate. It is saying that division would remain rather than one permanent empire replacing Rome.

View historical context

The dates associated with each empire are approximate and describe changes in political dominance, not instantaneous transitions. The traditional Christian interpretation identifies the four metals as Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, and Rome.

The Western Roman Empire lost political unity gradually. AD 476 is a traditional marker, while the Eastern Roman Empire continued for centuries.

WHEN BELIEF CARRIED A COST

The Soldier Who Trusted the Prophecy

During World War II, a German soldier named Franz Hasel knew Daniel 2. His story is told in A Thousand Shall Fall, written by his daughter Susi Hasel Mundy with Maylan Schurch.

Franz believed Hitler would not permanently unite Europe because Daniel said the divided kingdoms would not remain joined.

Sharing that belief within the German military was dangerous.

According to the family’s published account, Franz presented the prophecy to his commanding officer and two other officers. One had extensive historical knowledge and confirmed the succession of empires.

The prophecy influenced the commander’s decisions. Near the end of the war, he had conserved enough fuel to move his company westward. The men surrendered to American forces rather than being captured farther east.

Daniel 2 was not merely interesting information to Franz.

He trusted it when trust carried a cost.

About the source

The Franz Hasel account is a published family testimony rather than an independent military history. Presenting it with the phrase “According to the family’s published account” preserves the story’s value without claiming more verification than the source provides.

THE DECISION

The Enemy Closer Than We Think

Studying the failures of kingdoms is easier than allowing God to examine our hearts.

I see myself in David’s story. David trusted God against Goliath and while Saul pursued him. Later, he made devastating choices and discovered that his deepest enemy was not always outside him.

The greater battle was within his own heart.

I eventually discovered the same thing. The world could offer distraction, achievement, and temporary pleasure. But it could not forgive me or remake me.

I did not simply need better circumstances.

I needed a changed heart.

The God who revealed the mystery to Daniel is not merely proving that He understands history.

He is inviting us to trust Him with our lives.
